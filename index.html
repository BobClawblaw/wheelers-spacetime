Curved space

A slice of space at one instant, lifted into an extra dimension so its curvature can be seen (Flamm's paraboloid). The gold orbit does not close: each perihelion lands further round, the same effect Einstein used to explain Mercury. The dashed ellipse is Newton's orbit with the same closest and farthest points.

Cyan pulses are light passing the mass. Close passes bend more; inside 3√3 M the light spirals in and never escapes.

Spacetime (x, y, t)

Drop one space dimension and let time run upward. The planet's history becomes a helix of a world line, the mass a vertical column. Beads mark one tick of each clock: the clock held at r = 4 ticks far less often than the one at r = 30. That is gravitational time dilation.

The cones on the top slice are where light can go next. Near the mass they narrow, most of all toward the outside.

Gravitational waves

Two masses spiral together. Their changing quadrupole sends ripples of strain outward at the speed of light, with twice the orbital frequency, rising in pitch and amplitude to the merger: the chirp LIGO first heard in 2015.

The ring of free particles above stretches one way and squeezes the other. Real strains are about 10⁻²¹; these are exaggerated beyond measure.

Newton's gravity remains an excellent approximation in weak fields and at low speeds: lower the mass and the gold orbit closes onto the dashed ellipse. Exact Schwarzschild geodesics, integrated with RK4 in the orbit's angle; time-like and null paths share one code path except for the 1/L² term.
